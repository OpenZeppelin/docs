---
title: Best Practices for Account Holders
---

These practices are for anyone who holds a confidential account, and for the wallet developers who choose defaults
on their behalf. The wallet carries out most of them: [Wallet Integration](/stellar-contracts/tokens/confidential/wallet-integration)
states what a wallet must do, and this page states what a holder should do and why. The
[Privacy Model](/stellar-contracts/tokens/confidential/privacy-model) explains what the chain makes public.

## Before Registering

- **Choose the auditor deliberately.** The `auditor_id` picked at registration can never change. That auditor sees
  the amount of every transfer the account sends or receives and, from the account's first spend onward, its
  balances. The choice is public too. Find out who holds the auditor key and under which rules it may be used.
- **Know what your keys derive from.** When the wallet derives the account's keys from its Stellar signing key,
  that key controls both spending and viewing, and the account's keys can't be rotated: registration is single-use,
  so replacing a compromised signing key means registering a new address and moving the funds. Export the spending
  secret as a backup before the account receives funds.
- **Don't expect anonymity.** Your address appears in every operation you take part in. The confidential token hides
  amounts and balances, not whom you deal with.

## Keeping Amounts Private

- **Keep value inside the confidential layer.** Deposit and withdrawal amounts are public, and a balance built only
  from them is public too. Pay other holders with confidential transfers instead of withdrawing to the underlying
  token and depositing again.
- **Don't mirror amounts at the boundary.** Withdrawing exactly what you received, or depositing exactly what you are
  about to send, reveals the hidden amount. Prefer common amounts for deposits and withdrawals, and don't tie their
  timing to specific transfers.
- **Expect counterparties to know.** The recipient of a transfer learns its amount, and so does the auditor on each
  side.
- **Never share your viewing key.** It reveals your whole history and the openings of every transfer you sent, and
  since it is bound to your account at registration, it can't be revoked. To prove one fact, such as the amount of a
  payment or that a balance is above a threshold, use
  [Selective Disclosure](/stellar-contracts/tokens/confidential/selective-disclosure).
- **Prove locally.** A remote prover sees your spending key, your balance, and the amount of every proof it builds.
  Opt in to one only if you would trust it with your entire confidential history.

## Protecting Your Balance

The chain stores only commitments. The opening of each balance, which every spend needs, lives in your wallet, and
the wallet rebuilds it from your keys and your account's event history when it loses its local state.

- **Back up what recovers your keys.** That is the Stellar signing key for an account whose keys derive from it, and
  the root or the exported spending secret otherwise. Losing it means losing the funds.
- **Keep old signing keys.** If you rotate the signer that controls your address, keep the previous key: an account
  whose keys derive from it can't be re-derived without it.
- **Sync regularly.** Stellar RPC keeps events only for a limited window. A wallet that relies on RPC alone must sync
  at least once per window, because credits that age out before it applies them are lost unless an
  [archive](/stellar-contracts/tokens/confidential/indexing) holds them. Prefer wallets that use one.
- **Merge regularly.** Received funds become spendable only after a merge, and regular merges also shorten the history
  a recovery has to replay.
- **Resync before spending after a warning.** A wallet that reports a mismatch between its local balance and the
  on-chain commitment has missed or misapplied an event. Spending from that state fails.

### Recovery Scenarios

| Situation | What recovers the account |
| --- | --- |
| Device lost, keys derived from the signing key | The signing key, plus the event history from RPC or an archive |
| Device lost, keys from a raw root or an imported secret | The backup of that root or secret, plus the event history. Without the backup, the funds can't be spent. |
| Signing key rotated off the address and discarded | Only a previously exported spending secret |
| Wallet unsynced for longer than the RPC window, no archive | Nothing, for the credits in the events that aged out |

<Callout type="warning">
Recover with the same wallet software that created the account where possible. The conformance vectors for key
derivation don't exist yet, so two wallets are not guaranteed to derive the same account from the same backup. See
[Requirements](/stellar-contracts/tokens/confidential/wallet-integration#requirements).
</Callout>

## Working with Spenders

- **Escrow only what the spender needs, for as long as it needs it.** The allowance is all a compromised spender can
  take, and the expiry bounds how long it can take it.
- **Check the expiry before delegating.** `set_spender` accepts a `live_until_ledger` that has already passed, which
  creates a delegation that is expired from the start.
- **Revoke expired delegations.** Expiry stops the spender but doesn't return the escrow; only `revoke_spender` does.
  An escrow that nobody touches for long enough is also archived, and must be restored before it can be revoked.
- **Remember that delegations are public.** Each owner-spender pair, its expiry, and every transfer the spender makes
  on your behalf are visible on-chain.
- **Plan how to disclose spender transfers.** You can't produce a sender-side disclosure for a transfer your spender
  made: ask the spender, or your auditor. See [Spenders](/stellar-contracts/tokens/confidential/spenders#auditing-and-disclosure).
